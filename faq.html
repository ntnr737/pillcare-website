Getting started 10

PillCare Reminder is a medication management app that helps you build consistent medicine routines. It sends smart reminders, tracks your doses, and lets you share your progress with family caregivers.
PillCare uses Google Sign-In — tap the "Sign in with Google" button and select your Google account. No separate password needed. Your account is tied securely to your Google account.
After signing in, tap the + button on the Today screen or go to the Treatment tab and tap "Add medication". Enter the medicine name, dosage, and when you need to take it. It takes less than a minute.

PillCare works offline for basic dose marking — your doses are saved locally and sync when you reconnect. Reminders, nearby search, AI insights, and caregiver alerts require an internet connection.
During first launch, you'll be asked for your name (pre-filled from Google), birth year, gender, and optional phone number. Location is detected automatically for emergency services. You can update all this later in My Profile.

Reminders 8

Check these in order: (1) Notifications are enabled in phone Settings → Apps → PillCare → Notifications, (2) Battery saver or Doze mode is not restricting background activity, (3) Do Not Disturb is off or PillCare is exempt, (4) Notifications are enabled inside the app under My Profile.
PillCare uses your phone's default notification sound for standard reminders. Medication reminders use the maximum priority channel so they make a sound and appear on the lock screen even on silent mode (if your phone allows).
Reminders appear at the exact time you scheduled the dose. A take button is active from 2 hours before the scheduled time until 4 hours after — outside this window, the dose is locked to prevent logging errors.
Yes — if a medication is taken multiple times per day, each scheduled time gets its own reminder. You can also set a second follow-up time for any dose if you tend to miss the first alert.
PillCare's medication channel uses Android's highest notification priority (PRIORITY_MAX). On most Android phones this bypasses silent mode. However, this depends on your phone manufacturer's implementation — some phones (especially MIUI, ColorOS) may still block it.
Yes. Go to My Profile and toggle "Enable notifications" off. This pauses all reminders but keeps your medications and history intact. Turn it back on when you're ready.
PillCare uses your device's local time. If you travel across time zones, reminders will adjust automatically to local time — so a "9 AM" reminder will fire at 9 AM in whatever time zone your phone is in.
In the Treatment tab, tap on Yoga Therapy and then tap "Start Routine". A time picker appears — select your preferred practice time. A daily notification will be scheduled at that time every day. You can change or cancel it from the same screen.

Dose tracking 9

On the Today screen, find the dose under the correct time section (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Night) and tap the "Take" button. The dose is marked with the exact time you tapped it. This is permanent and cannot be changed.
No. Once a dose is marked as taken, it is permanent. This is by design — your medication history is a medical record and should not be editable after the fact. If you made a mistake, contact support@pillcare.in and we can review your case.
If you don't mark a dose within the active window (2 hours before to 4 hours after scheduled time), it is automatically marked as missed. Missed doses affect your adherence percentage and may trigger a caregiver alert. Always consult your doctor about what to do when you miss a dose — PillCare does not give medical advice on this.
The Take button is only active within a window — 2 hours before the scheduled time until 4 hours after. Outside this window, the button shows a lock icon with a countdown (" in 3h"). This prevents accidental early logging.
PillCare focuses on real-time dose tracking. You cannot retroactively log doses for past dates — this keeps your history accurate and trustworthy.
Adherence is the percentage of scheduled doses you've taken. 100% means you took every dose on time. It is calculated as: (doses taken ÷ total doses scheduled) × 100. A higher percentage means a more consistent routine.
Your streak counts consecutive days where you took all your scheduled doses. Missing any dose in a day resets the streak to zero. The streak is shown on the Today screen and in Progress.
Yes — Premium users can export dose history as a CSV file from the History screen. This is useful for sharing with your doctor. The export includes medication name, scheduled time, actual time taken, and status for each dose.
Free plan users can view the last 7 days of history. Premium plan users can view 90 days. Your full history is stored securely on our servers — you can request a complete export at any time by emailing support@pillcare.in.

Caregiver 8

The caregiver feature lets you connect a family member or trusted person — like a parent, child, or spouse — so they can receive WhatsApp alerts about your daily medication adherence. They do not need to install PillCare.
Go to the Support tab → Connect via WhatsApp. Enter your caregiver's phone number (with country code). They'll receive a WhatsApp message from our system when you miss doses. You can test it immediately with the "Send test alert" button.
Caregiver alerts are sent once daily at 10 PM IST with a summary of your day — how many doses were taken, missed, and overall adherence. An alert is only sent if you missed at least one dose that day.
No. Caregiver alerts are sent via WhatsApp. As long as your caregiver has WhatsApp installed on their phone, they'll receive the alerts. They don't need a PillCare account.
Free plan users can connect 1 caregiver. Premium plan users can connect unlimited caregivers. Alerts are sent to all connected caregivers simultaneously.
Go to Support → Connect via WhatsApp → clear the phone number field and save. The caregiver will stop receiving alerts immediately.
Caregivers receive a simple daily summary: how many doses were taken vs missed, your overall adherence percentage, and an encouraging message. They do not receive medication names, dosages, or any other personal health details unless you share them directly.
Check that: (1) The number is entered correctly with country code (e.g. +91 for India), (2) WhatsApp is installed and active on that number, (3) The number has not blocked our service. You can also send a test alert from the Support tab to verify the connection.

Nearby 6

The Nearby screen uses your GPS location to find pharmacies, diagnostic labs, and hospitals close to you. Results come from Google Places API (when available) or OpenStreetMap as a free fallback. Tap any result to get walking directions in Google Maps.
You can access Nearby from the Treatment tab → "Find Nearest Medical Store" at the bottom, or from the Support tab → Quick actions. You can search for pharmacies, diagnostic labs, or hospitals and clinics.
Try increasing the search radius — use 2km or 5km instead of 500m. If you're in a rural area, OpenStreetMap coverage may be limited. You can tap "Open Google Maps instead" to search directly in Google Maps.
Free users see up to 5 nearby results. Premium users see up to 20 results. Results are sorted by distance from your current location.
Yes. Tap "Directions" on any nearby result and it opens Google Maps with walking directions to that location. You can switch to driving or transit in Google Maps.
Nearby requires location permission to work. If you denied it, go to phone Settings → Apps → PillCare → Permissions → Location → Allow while using the app, then try again.

Yoga & wellness 8

Yoga Therapy recommends specific yoga poses and breathing exercises based on the medications you're taking. It maps your medicines to likely health conditions and suggests complementary yoga routines from verified AYUSH sources including Swami Ramdev and Sadhguru's channels.
The yoga recommendations are sourced from Ministry of AYUSH guidelines and trusted yoga practitioners. They are complementary wellness suggestions, not medical treatments. Always consult your doctor before starting any new exercise programme, especially if you have a health condition.
PillCare analyses your medication list to identify likely conditions (e.g. diabetes medications → recommends Mandukasana and Kapalbhati). Recommendations are condition-based, not diagnosis-based. We use a curated database of medication-condition-yoga mappings.
Tapping a yoga routine opens a verified YouTube video from official sources like the Ministry of AYUSH, Swami Ramdev, or Sadhguru's channels. The video opens in YouTube or your browser.
In the Treatment tab → Yoga Therapy → tap "Start Routine" → select your preferred time from the clock picker → confirm. You'll receive a daily notification at that time. To cancel, tap "Start Routine" again and dismiss the reminder.
Poses include Mandukasana (frog pose for diabetes), Pawanmuktasana (wind-relieving pose), Anulom Vilom (alternate nostril breathing), Kapalbhati (breathing exercise), Bhujangasana (cobra pose), Ardha Matsyendrasana (spinal twist), Setu Bandhasana (bridge pose), Surya Namaskar, and more.
The free plan includes basic yoga recommendations. The full yoga library with all poses, detailed instructions, and personalised routines is available on the Premium plan.
These are companion tracking tabs alongside your medications. Measurements lets you log health readings (blood pressure, blood sugar etc). Activities tracks physical activity. Mood lets you log your daily emotional wellbeing. All help you see the complete picture of your health routine.

Progress & insights 7

The Progress screen shows your daily adherence bar chart, current streak, 7-day average (free) or 30-day average (premium), and a list of recent dose history. It also shows an AI-generated wellness insight based on your adherence patterns.
AI insights analyse your adherence patterns, the medications you're taking, and your activity to generate personalised wellness tips. They are generated using an AI language model (we use Groq). Insights are updated weekly on the free plan and daily on Premium. They are suggestions only — not medical advice.
